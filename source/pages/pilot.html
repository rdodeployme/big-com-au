How a pilot runs.

Six steps, from the first site check to the report. Pilot homes run alongside your normal homes so there's a fair comparison.

  1. Site check

    We confirm where the station sits on your standard lot and driveway, and sort any permits.

  2. Baseline

    We record what your current skips cost and recover, so there's something real to compare with.

  3. Station on at slab

    The station arrives with the slab pour and stays until handover.

  4. Trade induction

    A one-page sheet and a five-minute walk-through for supervisors and trades.

  5. Weighed change-overs

    Each bay is emptied and weighed at our facility, so every tonne is recorded by stream.

  6. Pilot report

    Tonnes recovered, contamination, cost against your baseline, and what to change before scaling up.

Who does what.

The builder runs the site. Recycle Group runs the station and everything after it leaves the lot.

The builder

  • Picks the pilot homes and supervisors
  • Adds the BIG Standard to pilot subcontracts
  • Calls change-overs when a bay is full
  • Shares current skip costs for the baseline

Recycle Group

  • Supplies, delivers and changes over the station
  • Inducts supervisors and trades
  • Weighs and processes every bay by stream
  • Writes the pilot report

Before you start.

What builders usually ask.

How many homes is a pilot?

Enough to compare fairly with your normal homes. We agree the number with you at the site check, based on how many homes you start each month and in which estates.

Do we have to stop using our current skip contractor?

No. Pilot homes use BIG. Your other homes carry on as normal, which is what gives the report its baseline.

What does it cost?

Pilot pricing is set at the site check, once we know the homes, the estates and the service. The report then compares the real cost against your current skips.

Where do you operate?

Tell us where you build and we'll confirm whether the pilot can run there.

What can't go in?

Asbestos, fibro, chemicals, paint, solvents, liquids, batteries, gas bottles and any other hazardous waste.

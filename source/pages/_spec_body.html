Build it to this.

A low hook-lift skid carrying eight stand-alone 4 mm steel bins, four per side, no higher than 2 m and no longer than 6.3 m. Each bin has a hard lid, a drop-down front flap and a trap door on its right-hand side for emptying. Change the bin sizes below and every drawing, dimension and quantity updates. 1. Set the bin sizes

Four bins per side. Each side of the skid is ten 585 mm modules, and a bin is one to six modules wide. Use + and − to resize a bin, the arrows to move it along its side, and ⇄ to swap it with the bin opposite. Free modules show as gaps until you give them to a bin.

2. General arrangement

Drawn to scale from the layout above. All dimensions in millimetres. Heights are from the ground with the bins on the skid; a bin standing on the ground is 100 lower. Not for construction until the fabricator's shop drawings are approved.

3. Bin schedule

Bin sizes follow from the module count. Tare is an estimate from 4 mm plate plus 25% for the base, pockets and frames. Full load uses loose densities from BRANZ REBRI (see how it's sized). Where a full bin would pass its rated load, the schedule gives a painted fill line.

Bin width = modules × 585 − 20 (10 mm clearance each side). Depth 1,150. Height from the bin base: 1,900 at the front, 1,800 at the back. Internal: less 4 mm plate each side, floor 400 above the ground on the skid.

4. Specification

What each part has to do. Sections and details are proposed starting points. The fabricator and a structural engineer set the final ones.

4.1 Fixed requirements and how this design meets them

  • Design basisNo higher than 2,000. Top of every bin is 2,000 at the front and 1,900 at the back. Hook frame, lights and siren stay under 2,000.
  • Design basisNo longer than 6,300, four bins per side. Skid 6,300 overall: 150 rear zone for the rollers, 5,850 of bins (ten 585 mm modules), 300 hook zone.
  • Design basisDrop-down front flap from 1,200 to the 2,000 mark. Bottom-hinged at 1,200, closes against the lid lip at 2,000.
  • Design basisFork pockets 200 off the ground, bins behind a 100 rail. Pocket underside 200 above the bin base: 200 off the ground when a bin stands on the ground, and 300 on the skid, so forks pass 100 clear over the skid's 100 rail.
  • Design basisHard lid and a side trap door to empty. Fixed 4 mm lid. Trap door on the right-hand side as you face the flap, hinged along its top edge.
  • Design basis100 mm fall so the lid can't hold water. The fall runs from 2,000 at the front down to 1,900 at the back, into the gap between the two rows. A rise toward the back would take the lid over 2,000 with the flap at 2,000, so the fall runs the other way. It also keeps run-off away from the trades at the flaps.
  • Design basis4 mm all sides. Floor, front, back, both sides, lid, flap and trap door in 4 mm plate.

4.2 Overall dimensions

    4.3 Hook skid

    • Design basisLow skid 6,300 × 2,450, deck top 100 above the ground, with a hook A-frame in the front 300 and rear rollers in the back 150.
    • Design basisA 100 high retaining rail along both outer edges and down the centre, the full length of the bin zone, plus a rear end stop. Bins sit inside the rails and can't slide or walk off.
    • ProposedTwo longitudinal runners and cross-members under a plate deck, with drain holes along the centre so lid run-off between the rows drains through.
    • To confirmRunner spacing, hook height (about 1,450 shown), roller size and whether a 100 deck suits the operator's hook trucks. If the fleet needs taller runners, every height on this sheet rises by the same amount, so the bins would have to drop to stay under 2,000.

    4.4 Bins

    • Design basisEight stand-alone steel bins, 1,150 deep, one to six modules wide. Any bin can be swapped for a spare of the same width.
    • Design basisBase 300 high: two runners under the side walls, then two fork pockets 200 wide × 100 high running front to back, underside 200 above the bin base. Floor 300 above the bin base (400 above the ground on the skid).
    • Proposed4 mm plate on a folded or light angle frame at the corners and openings so the walls stay flat when full. Fork pocket centres about 55% of the bin width, between 300 and 1,000.
    • ProposedEvery bin rated to the same load (selected above), so any bin can take any stream. Rated load and fill line painted inside.
    • To confirmStability of one-module bins (565 wide, 1,900 high) on forks and when tipping. A wider fork carriage or a clamp may be needed.

    4.5 Hard lid and water

    • Design basisFixed 4 mm lid, welded, falling 100 from the front edge (2,000) to the back edge (1,900).
    • ProposedLid lip over the flap top so rain can't drive in at the flap. Back edge overhangs the back wall so water drips into the centre gap, not down the wall.
    • ProposedFloor drain hole with a plug at the lowest corner so plasterboard and cardboard can be drained before tipping.

    4.6 Drop-down front flap

    • Design basisOne flap per bin, bottom-hinged at 1,200, closing to 2,000. Clear opening the bin width less 70, about 780 high.
    • ProposedTwo check chains or stays hold the flap level as a loading ledge for sheets and offcuts. Unclip them and the flap hangs straight down against the front.
    • ProposedFolded edges for stiffness, rubber buffers where it meets the front, and no pinch point at the hinge.

    4.7 Trap door and emptying

    • Design basisTrap door in the right-hand side wall (as you face the flap), hinged along its top edge, about 1,030 wide × 1,340 high, from 460 to 1,800 above the ground.
    • Design basisTo empty, a forklift with a rotator takes the bin off the skid by its fork pockets and turns it to the right. The trap door swings open under gravity and the load slides out onto its own pile. The lid never comes off.
    • ProposedTwo over-centre latches with safety pins along the bottom edge. Released only at the facility. On site the doors face the next bin, so they can't be opened. Doors on end bins face outward and need a keyed latch.
    • To confirmThe facility's forklift, rotator rating and fork spread for the narrowest and heaviest bins.

    4.8 Locking, lights and alarm

    • Design basisOne system run from one phone app over Bluetooth: a lock on every flap, three motion-sensor lights and an alarm that arms itself on a schedule. No rods, bars or padlocks.
    • Design basisLocks. A battery smart latch on each bin's lid lip holds the flap shut. Bins come off the skid, so the locks are self-contained with no wiring. One tap locks or unlocks all eight, and every opening is logged.
    • Design basisSensor lights. Two LED floodlights on an arm at the top of the hook frame, one raking along each side, and one on a rear post. All under 2,000 and fixed to the skid, so they stay when bins are lifted off.
    • Design basisAlarm. The siren arms for chosen hours, for example 6 pm to 6 am on weekdays and all weekend. While armed, a flap opening, a forced lock or a tamper on the controller sets it off and the lights flash.
    • ProposedController, battery, siren and keyed override on the hook frame. Lights angled down so they don't shine into neighbours' windows. Siren run time limited, with automatic reset.
    • To confirmBluetooth only reaches a phone nearby. For alarm alerts to the supervisor's phone when nobody is on site, the controller needs a 4G module and a data plan.
    • To confirmLock, light, siren and controller hardware, IP ratings, battery life and charging, siren volume and run time against the state's noise rules.

    4.9 Finish and marking

    • ProposedBins, lids, flaps and doors charcoal . Skid rails and accents copper #C2804F.
    • Design basisBin sign plate on the lower front of each bin, the bin width less 80 and about 840 high. Copper header with the bin number in black, the stream name in black capitals, then what goes in and what stays out.
    • Design basisFlap marking. Bin number in white and stream name in copper on every flap.
    • ProposedPlates in aluminium composite with UV-stable printed vinyl, list text at least 50 high and names 80 to 140 high. "BUILDBIN" on the hook-end plate. Rear post sign: BUILDBIN, alarmed after hours, no asbestos, chemicals or gas bottles.
    • To confirmPaint system and corrosion protection for site and facility use.

    4.10 Safety and compliance

    • To confirmSkid, hook interface, fork pockets and trap door hinges and latches designed and certified by a qualified structural engineer to the relevant Australian Standards, with rated loads marked.
    • To confirmLoaded mass within the truck's hook-lift rating. Eight 4 mm bins come to about the tare shown in section 1, before the skid.
    • ProposedTrap door latches that can't release on the skid or under a forklift until a safety pin is pulled. No sharp edges, guarded pinch points, no footholds.

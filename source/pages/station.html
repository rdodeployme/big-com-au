    The drawing.

    Side elevation, lid on and flaps closed. Bay lengths come straight from the sizing model, so this side shows timber, bricks and concrete, steel and plasterboard. The other four bays are on the far side.

    Concept dimensions in millimetres. Hook height, rail spacing and weight limits are set with the fabricator to suit standard hook-lift trucks.

    Built for a building site.

    Every detail answers a problem with skips on new home sites.

    A lid on everything

    The whole station is roofed. Plasterboard and cardboard stay dry enough to recycle, and polystyrene and wrap can't blow down the street.

    Flaps that become awnings

    Each bay's side flap hinges at the top and props open over the opening, so it keeps the rain off while a trade is loading.

    Numbers before words

    Big copper bay numbers and plain labels on both sides. The trade sheet, the supervisor and the facility all use the same numbers.

    Heavy bay in the middle

    Bricks and concrete weigh the most, so that bay sits near the centre of the body, over the rails, to keep the lift balanced.

    No bay too small to use

    The smallest bays are held at about 0.6 m long, so a trade can still drop an armful of polystyrene or steel offcuts in one go.

    One lift, all eight streams

    Because each bay is sized by the space its material takes, the bays fill at about the same rate. One hook-lift visit changes the whole station over.

    Every bay, explained.

    What goes in, what stays out, and where it goes next. The same guide is on the trade sheet in the site shed.

    Processing routes for each stream are proposed and confirmed for each region during the pilot.

    How a change-over works.

    Most homes need about one or two change-overs from slab to handover. Each one is a single truck visit.

    1. Station on at slab

      The station is dropped on the driveway crossover when the slab is poured.

    2. Trades fill their bays

      Each trade drops its offcuts and packaging into the matching bay before leaving site each day.

    3. Supervisor calls it

      When a bay reaches its fill line, the supervisor books a change-over.

    4. One truck, one visit

      A hook-lift truck takes the full station and drops an empty one in the same visit.

    5. Weighed bay by bay

      At the facility each bay is emptied and weighed separately, so every tonne is recorded by stream.

    6. Reported per home

      The builder gets tonnes recovered by stream for every home, ready for council plans and sustainability reports.

    Where it sits.

    One station takes the place of the row of skips a site would otherwise need. Where it goes on each lot, and which way the hook end faces for the truck, is worked out in the pilot site check against your standard lot, setback and driveway.

    • One footprint, about the size of a large skip
    • Hook end clear for the truck to reverse up
    • Loading sides reachable from the site
    • Any council permit needs sorted before delivery

    Never in the station.

    Asbestos · fibro · chemicals · paint and solvents · liquids · batteries · gas bottles · aerosols · any hazardous waste

    These are managed separately under each trade's own lawful arrangements. BIG doesn't accept them.

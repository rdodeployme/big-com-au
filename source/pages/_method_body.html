How the bays are sized

We let the numbers set the size.

Each bay's size is its share of the loose volume that building a new home produces. Here is the data, the conversion, and every assumption, so anyone can check it. Pilot weighbridge data replaces these estimates as it comes in.

The result.

Central estimate for an Australian brick-veneer, slab-on-ground home. Weight share is our blend of the published sources below. Space share uses measured loose unit weights.

"Built bay" applies a 0.6 m minimum bay length so polystyrene and steel can be loaded by hand; the extra room comes off the other bays in proportion. Bay volumes assume the 6.3 × 2.45 × 2.2 m concept body, about 31 m³ inside.

How we got there.

Six steps, all run in code. The same model drives the Blender station and the numbers on this site.

  1. Collect the splits

    Material shares by weight from three published sources, mapped onto the eight BIG streams.

  2. Blend for Australia

    NZ builds are mostly timber-clad. Australian homes add brick veneer and more concrete, so we carry more rubble than the NZ studies show.

  3. Convert to space

    Divide each stream's weight by its loose unit weight from the BRANZ REBRI conversion chart.

  4. Size the bays

    Each bay gets its share of the station, so all eight fill at roughly the same rate over a build.

  5. Set the floor

    No bay shorter than 0.6 m. The two rows are balanced so each side holds about half the volume.

  6. Replace with real data

    Every pilot change-over is weighed by stream. The split, and the bay sizes, update from that.

How much a home produces

A study of New Zealand detached homes measured 32.2 kg of construction waste per m² of floor area for single-storey homes and 24.8 kg/m² for double-storey (Domingo and Batty 2021, as reported by Low et al. 2024). Auckland Council puts an average house build at 4.5 tonnes. BRANZ cites about 4 tonnes. We use 32.2 kg/m² as the central rate, which gives 7.7 tonnes for a 240 m² home. Brick-veneer homes on slabs may run higher, so the site also shows a high case of 45 kg/m² (an assumption, not a measurement).

At 5.34 m³ of loose volume per tonne of mixed new-home waste, 7.7 tonnes is about 41 m³: around seven 6 m³ skips, or 1.6 change-overs of a BIG station filled to 85%.

What we assumed

  • Rubble at 30% of weight, between the NZ studies (near zero) and Your Home's general building waste table (37%). Assumption
  • Polystyrene at 13% of plastics, from the measured NZ build. Measured, NZ
  • Brick and concrete rubble at 1,100 kg/m³, between BRANZ's concrete rubble (1,165.56) and hardfill (995.46). Plastics at 55 kg/m³, a blend of soft plastic (36.96) and PVC (99.88). Metals at 250 kg/m³, between clean light gauge (194.2) and general metal (379.6). Assumption
  • Excavated soil is excluded. It is handled separately and never goes in the station.
